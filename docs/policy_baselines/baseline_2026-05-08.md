Policy: `unitree_rl_mjlab/deploy/robots/g1/config/policy/velocity/v0/exported/policy.onnx`
Pipeline: `g1_sim_rl_combo.ComboController`
  (post-policy arm override + arm-slice obs masking + Kp boost while overriding)
Runner: [g1_sim_demo/g1_sim_baseline_runner.py](../../g1_sim_demo/g1_sim_baseline_runner.py)
Sim: `unitree_mujoco/simulate_python/unitree_mujoco.py`, elastic band disabled
once policy was running.

Verdict thresholds:
  - PASS:     max|Δgz| ≤ 0.05 AND |final_gz + 1| ≤ 0.05
  - MARGINAL: max|Δgz| ≤ 0.15 AND |final_gz + 1| ≤ 0.15
  - FAIL:     anything worse, OR robot fell (gz crossed 0 at any sample)

`max|Δgz|` is the worst |gz - (-1)| over the trial window; `Δgz=0` is
perfectly upright, `Δgz=2` is fully upside down. `final gz` is sampled
5 s after gesture trigger (post-recovery). `max dq` is the peak abs
joint velocity across all 29 motors during the trial.

## Read-out

The split is unambiguous: **standing balance + arm gesture** is the
failure mode; **walking + arm gesture** already works. The velocity
policy can stand at cmd=0 indefinitely (verified at 60 s in earlier
headless tests, see `g1_sim_rl_combo._tick` comments dated
2026-05-06), and it can absorb arm perturbations while walking, but
it cannot do both at once — its leg torques at cmd=0 are nearly zero,
so any non-trivial arm perturbation is unbalanced.

Implications for option 2:

1. **Training scenarios must include cmd=(0,0,0) with arm-disturbance
   active.** The velocity training task (`Unitree-G1-Flat`) already
   samples cmd=0 occasionally for the standing-mode pose reward, but
   never together with a meaningful arm motion.

2. **The arm-slice obs mask is *not* the failure root cause.** Even
   while walking with masking on, the policy already balances all 9
   gestures. The standing failure is about *no leg torque*, not about
   the policy seeing wrong joint_pos_rel. Option 2 should keep the
   masking trick or replace it with full arm-state exposure — both
   architectural choices are viable per these results — but the
   *required* training change is the standing-with-disturbance
   scenarios.

3. **Curriculum: walking → standing.** Start training with
   non-zero velocity command + gesture event firing, gradually
   reduce velocity toward 0. The policy already wins on the
   non-zero side; pull it down to 0 with the same disturbance.

4. **Asymmetric gesture pairs (`wave_right` vs `wave_left`)** suggest
   the velocity policy v0 has unbalanced training across leg-pair
   ground contacts. Worth keeping in mind when designing reward
   shaping: a left/right-symmetric reward term may help.
